# Design context and decisions

For an English-speaking reader curious about ancient geometry, with no Sanskrit
prerequisite. The job is to try a short historical instruction, inspect what it
preserves, and find the printed evidence. Tone: exact, tactile, exploratory.

The signature is a taut cord swung from a square's corner toward the east,
with the lost corners and gained circular segments drawn as actual areas.
The return construction must stay visible beside its misleadingly close result.
No invented temple silhouette, decorative mandala or pseudo manuscript background.

Palette: washed blue #e7eef2, deeper blue cloth #d5e3e9, ink #173c51,
oxide #a6462c for cut-away area, river #226579 for added area, chalk #fbfcf9.
Newsreader sets short English headings; IBM Plex Sans sets explanations and
measurements; Noto Serif Devanagari sets the Sanskrit source. All fonts local.
Desktop uses a large proportional plan beside the instruction rail. On phones,
steps precede the drawing, and the return drawing follows the area comparison.
Controls, words and source pages remain usable without hover. Reduced motion
needs no animation fallback: the construction advances only on explicit input.

The blue drawing ground was chosen over the cream and terracotta treatment
already common across these ancient-learning projects. No repeating card grid.
A short opening leads directly into the experiment; source detail follows it.
